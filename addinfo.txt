Retail investor —— индивидуальный НЕ профессионал, который покупает и продает securities.

Turnover —— the total money a business collects from customers before subtracting any operating expenses, taxes, or production costs. 

Stock-level characteristics —— specific, measurable traits of an individual company (e.g., P/E ratio, trading volume, company size) used to explain its unique price movements.

Macroeconomic variables —— road, economy-wide indicators (e.g., interest rates, GDP growth, inflation) that affect the entire stock market simultaneously.

Industry dummies —— statistical placeholders (coded as 1 or 0) used in research models to isolate and control for the natural, baseline differences between specific business sectors (e.g., Tech vs. Utilities).

Abnormal turnover ratio (atr) —— это отклонение реального показателя оборачиваемости от его исторического, отраслевого или ожидаемого нормального значения.

Cross-section of stock returns —— comparing many different stocks at a single point in time (or over a specific period).

Time-series of stock returns —— racking one specific stock (or the entire market index) across many different time periods.

Out-of-sample R² (R-squared) —— a statistical metric that measures how well a predictive model performs on new, unseen data.

Valuation ratio —— a financial metric used to determine whether a stock (or the overall market) is cheap, expensive, or fairly priced relative to its underlying business fundamentals.
